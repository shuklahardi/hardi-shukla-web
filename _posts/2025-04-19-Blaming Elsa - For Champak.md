Capturing Guilt and Innocence: Illustrating "Blaming Elsa"

![My Image](/assets/images/Blamingelsa/blamingelsa2.jpg)

The focus is on the stress of copying versus the joy of sharing a story. Grandma (Dadi) appears with a soft, happy face, narrating freely from her imagination. In contrast, Tarun’s struggle is shown through his body language; his fingers clutch a pencil tightly in desperation. An angry, tired expression reveals how he loses his own thoughts by trying too hard to copy hers.

![My Image](/assets/images/Tarunstale/Tarun'stales3.PNG)

Above scene captures Tarun’s lowest moment at the school assembly. The composition places him alone in a bright spotlight, making him look small and emphasizing a "blank" feeling. His posture includes sloping shoulders and fingers nervously touching his shirt, which shows a clear lack of confidence. The surrounding crowd’s laughter makes his frozen pose stand out, highlighting his isolation.

![My Image](/assets/images/Tarunstale/Tarun'stales4.PNG)

The color palette blends a classic tale with a sad reality. Earthy browns and muted "down tones" give the art an old-world Alibaba feel. These soft, heavy colors reflect Tarun’s sadness, replacing happy glows with a mood that feels more emotional and defeated.
